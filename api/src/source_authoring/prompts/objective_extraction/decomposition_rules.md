# Objective Decomposition Rules

Prompt ID: `source-objective-decomposition-rules`

Prompt Version: `v2`

Apply these rules as an executable rubric, not as tags to display to a reviewer.

- D-1: Generate enabling objectives, not broad terminal outcomes or topic labels.
- D-2: Ground every candidate in precise source evidence and preserve lineage.
- D-3: Consider explicit objectives as signals, but verify them against source substance.
- D-4: Use descriptions of learner performance, practice, assessment, or workplace use
  as strong decomposition signals.
- D-5: Use headings and structure to find boundaries; never simply turn headings into
  objectives.
- D-6: Treat direct procedural, decision, risk, and error-prevention content as strong
  signals when they support realistic Deloitte practitioner performance, but do not let
  action-oriented relevance displace supported retrieval or explanation performances.
  Practitioner framing alone does not establish higher cognitive demand.
- D-7: Treat worked examples, scenarios, exercises, questions, and feedback as evidence
  of intended learner performance rather than as objectives by themselves.
- D-8: Judge confidence from corroborating signals. Three or more independent signals
  normally support High, two Medium, and one Low unless evidence quality warrants a
  documented exception.
- D-9: Treat emphasis, repeated warnings, common errors, and SME emphasis as candidate
  signals.
- D-10: Use prompts and questions to infer cognitive demand without copying their verbs.
- D-11: For transcript-only candidates, default to no more than Medium confidence unless
  the intent is corroborated.
- D-12: Decode structured artifacts: flows, tables, diagrams, and handoffs may reveal
  performance and boundaries that captions omit.
- D-13: Cross-check a candidate against all supplied sources. Record corroboration and
  contradictions; do not silently broaden it.
- D-14: Account for source richness and authority. A sparse authoritative source may
  provide a strong signal, but record the source and signal type.
- D-15: Generate candidates from primary sources. Supplementary-only candidates are Low
  confidence and require verification.
- D-16: Each candidate must be atomic enough to occupy one content-kind × Bloom cell.
  Split independently learnable or assessable performances. Do not decide the cell here.
- D-17: Target roughly 5–15 minutes of active engagement as a granularity heuristic.
  Split an oversized objective; merge only narrow objectives that describe one coherent
  performance.
- D-18: Use prerequisites and postconditions to test the boundary: one clear entry state
  and one meaningful capability on completion.
- D-19: Flag dated or superseded evidence and exact existing coverage when known. Do not
  suppress a candidate without recording why.

Do not reduce candidate count to meet a quota. Keep low-confidence candidates visible
with their uncertainty. Decomposition discovers supported performance; it does not
summarize the document into topics and does not classify the candidate.

Boundary examples:

- “The publication explains incident response” is a topic/source description, not a
  learner performance. A supported performance such as distinguishing incident stages
  may be a candidate.
- “State each element and explain its purpose” contains retrieval and paraphrase. Split
  it when those can be learned and assessed independently; retain inseparable steps that
  together form one workplace performance.
