# Shared block — talking-head avatar medium (stripped on include)

The output is a single synthetic presenter speaking to camera over a solid
colour or one static image. There is no on-screen text, no diagrams, no b-roll,
no cutaways. What that means for the script:

- Speech carries 100% of the instruction. Never reference visuals that will not
  exist ("as you can see here", "on this slide"). Carry any structure vocally,
  and sparingly — a spoken list longer than three items does not land.
- Write direct address: speak to "you", and open each scene with the point, not
  a wind-up. Presenter energy comes from short declarative sentences, which
  also render more naturally through speech synthesis and lip-sync. Expand
  acronyms on first spoken use and keep numbers round and speakable.
- A static talking head is the lowest-visual-density video format; attention
  decays quickly. Scene boundaries are the only "camera cut" available — prefer
  scenes of roughly 45–90 seconds over one long monologue, and keep the total
  runtime short by design, not merely under the ceiling.
- The format excels at orientation, motivation, framing, and personal
  connective tissue. It is weak for dense procedural detail — when a scene's
  content is a wall of steps or data, tighten the script to the decision-level
  takeaways a presenter can credibly deliver to camera.
