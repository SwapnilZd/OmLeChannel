# RLO Authoring Guidance by Content Type and Bloom Level

POC2 authors RLOs for exactly five Content-Type × Bloom combinations. Content type
describes the knowledge structure; Bloom level describes what the learner must do
with it.

| Content type | Bloom level | Instructional signature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fact | Remember | Retrieve a precise fact from an appropriate cue. |
| Concept | Understand | Explain or distinguish a concept using critical attributes. |
| Concept | Apply | Classify a novel, unambiguous case using critical attributes. |
| Process | Understand | Explain or reconstruct stages, actors, dependencies, and transitions. |
| Procedure | Apply | Execute supported steps and decisions in a novel standard case. |

An objective that does not fit one of these combinations remains visible for review
but is not authorable in POC2. Do not force it into the closest cell.

## Guidance shared by all five cells

Each RLO teaches and assesses one independently learnable performance. Cover identifies
it; Setup establishes a credible moment of need; Teach supplies the stable knowledge;
Show makes expert thinking visible; Practice provides supported performance and useful
feedback; Check assesses the same performance independently in a new situation; and
Send-off prompts transfer without adding another objective.

Scenarios should contain only details that affect reasoning or credibility. Keep roles,
facts, dates, and consequences consistent. Vary surface context between Show, Practice,
and Check while preserving the same underlying demand. Do not invent organizational
policy, thresholds, responsibilities, or methodology.

Preferred activity shapes are guidance, not mandates. Use the strongest coherent
approximation the evidence and delivery format can support. Source integrity, a clear
instructional arc, and a valid assessment matter more than mechanically including every
suggested feature.

## Fact × Remember

- Teach the exact fact, scope, relevance, and distinctions that prevent confusion.
- Show how a practitioner recognizes the right retrieval cue.
- Practice retrieval with progressively less cue support when useful.
- Check precise retrieval from a new appropriate cue; do not turn the assessment into
  explanation or application.

## Concept × Understand

- Teach the definition, critical and variable attributes, examples, and near-miss
  non-examples.
- Show how attributes distinguish familiar instances.
- Practice explanation or distinction with explanatory feedback.
- Check the learner's understanding without requiring classification of a genuinely
  novel case.

## Concept × Apply

- Teach the critical attributes and a repeatable classification approach.
- Show a classification with the deciding attribute made visible.
- Practice novel, unambiguous cases with reduced support when the source supports
  credible variation.
- Check a new clear case and require both the classification and deciding attribute.

## Process × Understand

- Teach stages, actors, inputs, outputs, dependencies, handoffs, and transition
  triggers.
- Show the flow and the consequence of a commonly missed transition.
- Practice reconstructing the flow and associating actors and triggers with stages.
- Check process structure and transition logic without requiring failure diagnosis.

## Procedure × Apply

- Teach entry conditions, ordered steps, decision branches, rationale, common errors,
  exit conditions, and the intended output.
- Show each material action and why it is taken, including supported error recovery.
- Practice a novel standard case with diminishing prompts when credible variation is
  available.
- Check independent execution and decision-point reasoning in another standard case;
  do not require tailoring for a non-standard case.

## Quick review

Confirm that the objective, Teach, Practice, and Check all target the same approved
cell; the scenario is credible and evidence-bounded; feedback explains the deciding
basis; the Check uses a genuinely new case or cue; and the Send-off does not expand
scope.
